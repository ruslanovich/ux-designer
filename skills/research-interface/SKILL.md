# Research Interface

Investigate the question that affects the current interface task. Explain findings in the user's language. Use the host's available search and source-reading tools; this module requires no particular service, account, or independent agent. Respect explicit limits on browsing and access.

## Select the research contribution

1. Recover the task, audience, platform, requested result, constraints, available materials, and decisions the research could affect. Reuse relevant evidence already read in this project; check its fit and currency before repeating work.
2. Select an **orientation pass** for a broad redesign or unfamiliar product domain. Look for professional practice and typical failure modes before settling on a diagnosis. Equivalent evidence already read for this context can satisfy the pass; model confidence alone cannot.
3. Select **decision research** for a consequential choice, a gap in bundled guidance, a conflict between recommendations, or a detail dependent on current platform behavior. An explicit research request also applies. A routine local correction with adequate evidence needs no fresh literature review. [R40]
4. Formulate a bounded question and how its answer could change inspection or design. Read [research-method.md](references/research-method.md) for source selection, applicability, and stopping conditions.

## Investigate and apply

- Use relevant authorized project evidence and professional primary sources: original designer or researcher articles, design-team cases, practitioner guides, and studies. Use platform documentation and accessibility standards for their actual scope. Search beyond the bundled reading list when the question requires it.
- Search with the task, pattern, and constraints. Keep private product content out of public queries. A missing product fact or user-owned decision may need inspection or clarification.
- Open and read the substantive source before relying on it. Record its author or organization, title, URL, and relevant date or version. Search snippets alone do not establish its recommendation.
- Distinguish empirical findings, practitioner guidance, product cases, platform requirements, and visual references. Evaluate fit to the current audience, task, data scale, platform, and constraints; inspect conflicts and important limitations.
- Connect each material finding to a consequence: investigate a newly identified issue, change or confirm a choice, reject an unsuitable pattern, or retain an unresolved hypothesis. A supported decision to keep existing behavior is useful. A list of unrelated links does not complete the research.

## Close the question and return

Stop when the choice and its limits are clear, further sources repeat the same reasoning, or the remaining question needs product-specific observation, analytics, a prototype, or participants. Use no fixed source count. Continue feasible work with explicit assumptions when the remaining evidence is unavailable.

Return the question, applicable findings with sources and limits, their effect on the decision, and the next suitable check. Merge a concise delta into the existing `evidence`, `decisions`, `checks`, and `next` fields when useful; require no separate report or schema. Keep external findings separate from evidence about this product's users.

For a standalone research request, deliver the answer and its implications. In a broader workflow, return to `inspect-interface`, `plan-interface`, or `check-interface` according to the requested contribution. Route missing accessible material to inspection and a consequential user-owned choice to `clarify-interface`. Resolve module names through the host catalog, retaining the same plugin and its namespace when applicable. Honor explicit disablement and access restrictions.

If browsing or a source is unavailable, use the available material for a bounded result and identify only the consequential limit. Never claim an unperformed search, source read, user study, or runtime check. Do not infer audience understanding, conversion improvement, or universal optimality from professional guidance alone.
