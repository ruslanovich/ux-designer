# Frame an adaptive interview

Use this reference for R02, R03, R08, R09, and R10. Translate examples into the user's language and adapt them to the actual context. Treat them as question shapes rather than a required script.

## Separate question purposes

| Purpose | Offer | Record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Learn an existing fact | A neutral question, useful possibilities, free response, and “I don't know” | The reported answer and its source; retain unresolved details |
| Resolve ambiguous meaning | A concrete follow-up about the phrase or situation that affects a decision | The clarified meaning and affected interpretation |
| Choose a future direction | Alternatives, consequences, a grounded recommendation, free response, and delegation | A project decision and its basis; retain predictions as hypotheses |

Separate a question that mixes fact and choice. Distinguish who currently uses a product from whom to serve in its next version. Keep current and future experience explicit when the difference matters.

## Ask about a fact

Use a form such as:

> How do customers place an order today?
>
> Through messages; through a form; through a combination or another route. You can describe it in your own words, or say you don't know.

Choose possibilities from the context. Do not mark an account of reality as recommended. If the user cannot answer, preserve the unknown and explain a useful next action or temporary assumption separately.

## Recommend a future choice

When the user has named service inquiries as the landing-page goal, use a form such as:

> What should the first version include?
>
> **A service description and an inquiry path — recommended:** this supports the goal you named and requires fewer additional inputs.
>
> **An inquiry path and a price estimate:** this adds a pricing task and requires calculation rules.
>
> You can suggest another direction, combine parts, or ask me to choose a working version.

Tie the recommendation to what is actually known. Treat the choice as scope. Keep visitor preferences and expected conversion separate unless supported by corresponding evidence.

## Assess sufficiency by consequences

Identify the decision that would change if a missing answer took another plausible value. Use that dependency to choose a focused question or justify continuing with an assumption.

Proceed with a provisional model when the intended outcome and main path are coherent and unresolved parts can be bounded. Clarify incompatible goals or a material constraint when they prevent a coherent next step. Do not expand a local ambiguity into a full research exercise or require certainty about every audience characteristic.

If the user cannot know the fact, identify the affected decision, select a limited working direction within scope, and retain the condition for revisiting it. Keep explicit user boundaries intact. Do not count a nonresponse as confirmation.

Let the user describe their activity, desired result, constraints, and consequential preferences. Translate these into scenarios and structure yourself. Keep ordinary component selection inside the design work unless it changes a meaningful consequence or the user has a specific preference. Show a short summary rather than the whole evidence record after every answer.

## Use the foundations accurately

- Use [source 1, Erika Hall, Interviewing Humans](https://alistapart.com/article/interviewing-humans/) for flexible interviewing, context, and follow-up on vague answers. Treat adaptation to an agent conversation as this system's process choice.
- Use [source 2, Indi Young, A New Way to Listen](https://alistapart.com/article/a-new-way-to-listen/) to examine meaning and recognize assumptions. Keep reported experience distinct from the agent's reconstruction.
- Use [source 3, Maria Rosala, Task Analysis](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/task-analysis/) to connect goals, tasks, sequences, and conditions. Keep simulated paths distinct from observed behavior.
- Use [source 4, Kate Kaplan, Journey-Mapping Approaches](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/journey-mapping-approaches/) to distinguish current and future experience and preserve the role of hypotheses.
- Treat recommendation format, sufficiency, compact context, and module transitions as project requirements and process choices. Do not make field completion, an exhaustive interview, or approval at every step prerequisites for useful design work.
