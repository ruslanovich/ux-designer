# Interface quality coverage

## R38 — Survey relevant quality before narrowing the work

Apply to a broad audit or redesign, an unfamiliar interface, or a material gap in the initial diagnosis. For a small request, examine the affected area and consequential dependencies. This coverage model is the system's working method; it is not a validated universal checklist.

| Area | Inspect when it can affect the task |
| --- | --- |
| Tasks and product model | Outcomes, roles, objects, lifecycle states, handoffs, and necessary expert or infrequent work |
| Content and understanding | Needed information, terminology, reading order, onboarding, value explanation, and the evidence behind claims |
| Information architecture | Grouping, destinations, labels, navigation depth, finding, filtering, and relationships across areas |
| Interaction and recovery | Actions, selection scope, forms, feedback, waiting, empty or error states, correction, and return |
| Visual organization | Composition, alignment, spacing, typography, contrast, density, and prominence of important information or actions |
| Consistency and direction | Component treatments, terminology, visual language, and fit with the intended brand or supplied references |
| Conditions and access | Viewport, text expansion, input methods, assistive technology, localization, and platform conventions |

Use this breadth to discover relevant problems before selecting detailed references. Record a concrete concern, a useful strength, a reason an area is irrelevant, or an inspection limit where it changes the decision. Keep minor and already understood matters out of the handoff. The user need not see or fill a seven-part form.

## Choose what to inspect next

1. Anchor each important finding in a visible state, code location, supplied report, or explicit hypothesis. Describe the property and its consequence for the intended task; reserve claims about audience behavior for suitable observations.
2. Connect symptoms that may share a cause. Several weak screens can require a shared layout or navigation decision; a single poor state may need a local correction. Recommend scope from that relationship and the user's boundary.
3. Examine representative full screens and significant states when available. Use realistic content lengths and densities. Expand inspection when an observed discrepancy or dependency justifies it.
4. Compare distinct treatments of repeated components when consistency is in question. Preserve intentional differences that express different roles or states.
5. Pass a question to research-interface when orientation or professional guidance could reveal missing concerns or inform a consequential choice. Reuse prior evidence and explain the limit if research tools are unavailable.

Visible crowding, indistinguishable emphasis, or inconsistent treatments can justify a design correction without analytics. The diagnosis remains an expert assessment until the relevant audience outcome is observed. An uninspected state remains unknown.

## Check the inspection's usefulness

Confirm that the recommended scope could address the priority concerns, that required capabilities and useful strengths are represented, and that consequential gaps have an owner. A list of design terms or unsupported severity labels does not establish coverage. Keep an audit-only assignment at its requested endpoint.

## Professional basis

- [Brad Frost, Interface Inventory](https://bradfrost.com/blog/post/interface-inventory/) describes comparing distinct component treatments to expose unintended inconsistency and clarify redesign scope. Use it as practitioner guidance; a complete inventory is unnecessary for every task.
- [Kelley Gordon, 5 Principles of Visual Design in UX](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/principles-visual-design/) provides expert guidance on scale, hierarchy, balance, contrast, and grouping. Use those principles to inspect the visible composition, with contextual judgment and suitable checks.
- The seven-area taxonomy, scope recommendation, and handoff obligations are project decisions. They do not establish user comprehension, conversion gains, or complete accessibility.
